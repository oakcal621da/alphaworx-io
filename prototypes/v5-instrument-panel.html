The value gap · told through the programs that didn't

The consensus explanation — the models aren't good enough — describes a minority of the wreckage.

Our research desk audited the documented enterprise AI pilots that failed. The demo was crisp. The accuracy target was hit. Some shipped and ran for a year. Six chapters, every figure cited to source.

Chapter 01 — MD Anderson & IBM Watson, 2013–2016

A $62 million cancer engine that never saw a patient.

Built against the records system the hospital then migrated away from — and never rebuilt. Procured outside the process that keeps its underlying systems running. Whether the model was good became an academic question: the value was never reachable.

Gate 3 · the work never changed
70%
Of orders needed an off-site human at the best sites
Chapter 02 — Presto Automation, drive-thru voice AI

The "human-free" AI that wasn't.

The SEC found the voice system needed a remote human on 70% of orders at its best pilot locations and on 100% at most — and ordered the company to cease and desist. Regulators now price the operating gap directly, turning a soft governance failure into a hard cash one.

Gate 2 · measured against the wrong distribution
$408m
One-year write-down, Zillow Offers
Chapter 03 — Zillow, 2021

Scaled before the model's error was priced.

"Unpredictability in forecasting home prices far exceeds what we anticipated." The lesson generalises: compare the expected effect to the variance of the thing you're trying to move — not to zero — before you scale.

Gate 1 · value that wasn't ownable
85%
Of users reallocated their AI time savings
Chapter 04 — 25,000 Danish workers, survey linked to payroll

Capacity that is freed and never banked.

The largest study setting what workers say beside what employers actually paid them found precise null effects on earnings and hours — ruling out changes larger than 2%. Time saved is not money saved unless someone converts it. That is the dominant failure mode.

Gate 4 · nobody collected
19% slower
While estimating they were 20% faster
Chapter 05 — METR randomised trial, experienced developers

Self-reported productivity is measurably wrong — upward.

A 39-point gap between the felt effect and the measured one, inside the same people on the same tasks. The Atlanta Fed found the same shape in 750 CFOs. Business cases built on self-report inherit that gap at both levels.

Why anecdote can't be the evidence
2.5m uses
~16,000 documentation hours returned in one year
Chapter 06 — The Permanente Medical Group, ambient scribes

The programs that work are boring, narrow, and owned.

Software that listens to a clinical visit and drafts the note the doctor would otherwise type — attacking the single task clinicians most hated, with no headcount claim attached. Adoption concentrated where the pain was worst.
